# Investment summary

<span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">Airbus is the structurally advantaged half of a duopoly that is sold out into the 2030s. The question is not demand but how fast it can turn a ~9.3k-aircraft backlog into deliveries, and the July 2026 Business Update has shifted the equity story from "can they ramp?" to "how much cash does the ramp throw off?".</span>

1. **Market leader in the profit pool that matters.** ~55–57% of narrowbody deliveries, a backlog ~35% larger than Boeing's, and the A321 — the most sought-after aircraft in the market — as ~62% of A320-family deliveries.
2. **Visible earnings compounding.** Management targets adj. EBIT of <span style="color:rgb(255, 192, 0)">€12–13bn by 2029</span> (2025: ~€7.2bn sum of divisions, Commercial €5.5bn), a ~15% CAGR. The sell-side broadly reads this as a <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">floor, not a stretch</span>.
3. **Capital allocation inflection.** A €5bn buyback and a 30–50% payout target (~60% of 2026–29 cumulative FCF returned) mark the end of a decade in which cash was reabsorbed by industrial problems.
4. **What could go wrong.** Execution (Pratt & Whitney GTF engines, aerostructure quality), airline stress from jet fuel ≥$100/bbl, and USD exposure as hedge cover rolls off from 2028.

At ~11x 2028E EV/EBIT (MS) and a ~2.8% 2026E FCF yield rising to ~6.6% by 2030E (JPM), we think the market still prices Airbus as a ramp-execution story rather than as a compounding, cash-returning franchise. We start with a positive bias and see the Q4-26 delivery run-rate as the main checkpoint.

# 1. Role in the sector: one half of a duopoly, and the stronger half

## Industry structure
Large commercial aircraft is a <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">near-perfect duopoly</span> (Airbus / Boeing), with very high barriers to entry: certification, multi-decade programme economics, installed-base lock-in (pilot type ratings, spares, training) and a global supply chain that cannot be replicated. COMAC is the only credible long-dated challenger ([[Aerospace]]).

Demand is a function of traffic growth (~2x GDP, ~4–5% RPK long-term) and fleet replacement. Supply is the binding constraint: industry backlogs of <span style="color:rgb(255, 192, 0)">~15.7k aircraft = >10 years of production</span> ([[JEF A&D Weekly - Backlog growth led by Airbus Apr26]]). Pricing power therefore sits with the airframers, while the bottleneck sits one level down, with engine makers and aerostructure suppliers.

# 2. Earnings power: why the 2029 target looks conservative

**Unit economics of the A320.** Reverse-engineering the 2029 framework, Barclays derives <span style="color:rgb(255, 192, 0)">~€14m of profit per A320 at rate 75 and ~€16m per incremental delivery</span> ([[Barclays Airbus - From climb to cruise PT raised to EUR260 Aug26]]). BofA builds Commercial earnings power at full rate as A320 €12–13bn + A350 ~€2.5bn + A220 ~breakeven + A330neo/Services ~€0.7bn = €14–16bn pre-R&D, minus €3–4bn of R&D ≈ <span style="color:rgb(255, 192, 0)">€10bn</span> ([[BofA Airbus - Commercial set up for EUR10bn EBIT guide Jun26]]).

**Built-in cushions.** Morgan Stanley's own Commercial model reaches ~€10bn in 2029 on just <span style="color:rgb(255, 192, 0)">1,066 deliveries</span>, >10% below the ~1,200 implied by announced rates, with flat pricing, R&D at ~5% of sales and a €250m negative buffer ([[MS Airbus - Narrative shift yet to be priced Aug26]]). On top of that:
- **FX:** the guide assumes EUR/USD 1.22 vs a forward of ~1.19 and spot of ~1.15; <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">each 1c ≈ €250m of EBIT</span> ([[Hedges]]).
- **Widebody:** management describes the A350 profitability "sweet spot" as a 2030s story (launch-customer contracts and the A350F learning curve weigh on 2029), so there is little widebody upside in the guide.
- **Track record:** Airbus has historically framed medium-term targets conservatively ([[Barclays Airbus - The next chapter of the Airbus story Jul26]]).

# 3. Future prospects

## Near term (Q4-26 to 2027): delivery the ramp
- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">FY26 guide ~870 deliveries requires ~337 in Q4 (~112/month)</span>, i.e. 39% of the year in one quarter vs a ~42% historical Q4 weight. Achievable but tight; the YTD pace has been ~3pp behind the usual run-rate all year ([[Deliveries]]).
- The A320 rate target is <span style="color:rgb(255, 192, 0)">75/month by 2027</span>. All 10 FALs are operational and A321-capable. CFM LEAP (>500 engines/quarter) covers ~60% of the ramp; <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">Pratt GTF is the pacing item</span>, with a contract-breach dispute running in parallel ([[Supply Chain]], [[Pratt & Whitney GTF]]).
- Our concern: A320 first flights have drifted from ~50/month (Apr–May) to ~48 (Aug), and the Sep-26 Stelia stringer deviation affects ~250 aircraft still in production ([[Production Ramp]], [[Quality Issues]]). Rate 75 was first targeted for 2024, so the market will rightly apply a credibility discount until it is seen.

## Medium term (2027–2030): operating leverage + widebody
- **A350 beyond rate 12.** Management is evaluating rates "well beyond" 12, with a decision possibly before year-end, linked to an <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">A350-2000 stretch</span> (BofA assumes EIS ~2032). BofA sees market support for <span style="color:rgb(255, 192, 0)">16–18/month</span> and €3–5bn of A350 EBIT pre-R&D by 2032–34 ([[A350]]). The industry looks structurally short of widebody capacity even at planned rates.
- **A350F** EIS end-2027 targets the 777F replacement market, partly diluted by the FAA waiver allowing 35 more 777Fs ([[A350F]]).
- **Defence & Space.** Eurofighter output rising from ~10–13 to ~20+/year, MRTT taking share from Boeing's KC-46 (Italy €1.4bn for 6, a second Seville conversion line), the A400M now cash-positive, and the Bromo space JV with Thales/Leonardo (closing preferred in 2027). European defence spending grows ~11% organically 2026–30 ([[Defence and Space]]). BNP notes investors still pay little attention to this division — a potential SOTP angle.
- **Services:** targeted to reach ~10bn revenue by 2030 (roughly double today) at double-digit margins, with M&A as a lever ([[Services]]).

## Long term (2030s): the Next-Gen Single Aisle
The A320neo successor is targeted for launch around end of decade, with EIS in the second half of the 2030s. <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">The strategic intent is to rebalance OE vs aftermarket economics</span>: Airbus wants a share of the 30-year life-cycle value (engines, landing gear, brakes, nacelles) that today accrues to suppliers. RTX has confirmed preliminary talks, and an alignment with Rolls-Royce (which wants back into narrowbodies) is possible ([[Next Gen Single Aisle]]). If it succeeds, this would structurally re-rate Airbus toward the aftermarket-heavy peers. The main spend lands post-2030 (JPM's "R&D holiday"), which protects FCF through the guidance window.

| **Embraer (E2)** | Regional jets | Overlap with the A220-100 at the low end | Low. The bigger A220 issue is its own economics (below) |

**Programme-level competition**
- **A321neo vs 737-10:** Airbus' strongest moat — the A321 carries ~2/3 of the A320 backlog, and its rival is not yet certified.
- **A350 vs 787 / 777X:** the 787 leads on volume (~1.15k backlog, ~8/month). The 777X delays help the A350-1000 in the large-twin segment.
- **A220:** <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">a commercial success (1,109 orders) but not yet a financial one</span>. UBS questions whether it can be profitable with only two variants; an A220-500 stretch (+~25 seats) is being studied ([[A220]]).

**Illustrative cross-check (our math):** €12.5bn 2029 EBIT (guide midpoint) × 15x EV/EBIT ≈ €188bn EV, plus ~€14bn net cash ≈ €202bn equity, or ~€255/share on ~790m shares. Discounted two years at ~9% that gives <span style="color:rgb(255, 192, 0)">~€215 today, ~11% upside</span>, before any credit for FX or above-guide delivery volumes. # Bottom line

<span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">We start with a positive bias.</span> Airbus has the best product position in commercial aerospace (the A321), the largest backlog, a net-cash balance sheet, and for the first time a medium-term framework and capital-return policy that turn volume growth into per-share value. The debate has moved from demand to delivery. Over the next 6–12 months the stock should trade on (i) hitting ~870 in FY26 without a Q4 shortfall, (ii) visible progress toward A320 rate 75 (first flights back above ~50/month), and (iii) the A350 rate/A350-2000 decision. <span style="color:rgb(255, 192, 0)">Q3 results on 28 Oct</span> are the first test.
