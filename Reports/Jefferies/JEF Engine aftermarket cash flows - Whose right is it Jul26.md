**Jefferies — Engine Aftermarket Cash Flows: Whose Right Is It?, 23 Jul 2026 — Sector** · Sheila Kahyaoglu, Chloe Lemarie

# Summary
<span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">The engine business model (sell OE at a loss, earn on 25–30 years of aftermarket) is under pressure, and Airbus wants a share of the NSA aftermarket.</span> Both RTX and Airbus signalled openness to change, which makes this a key note for the [[Next Gen Single Aisle]] debate.

# Key takeaways
- Airbus targets <span style="color:rgb(255, 192, 0)">services revenue of €10bn by 2030</span>, with M&A as a driver; Lars Wagner (Commercial CEO) is discussing with aftermarket players how to merge the OE model with the 30-year aftermarket model for the NSA
- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">After the €5bn buyback, JEF sees a further ~€22bn of capacity for buybacks or M&A by 2030</span> while keeping the €10bn net cash buffer
- <span style="color:rgb(255, 192, 0)">New-build GTF loses ~$2m per engine</span> at RTX's 51% share (~$4m programme-level vs ~$3.75m ASP → ~$8m cost per engine)
- RTX commercial OE losses $1.14bn in 2026, peaking at ~$1.25bn in 2028–29
- LEAP broke even in 2025 (9 years after EIS); GE9X drives GE's OE losses peaking in 2027
- RTX management open to smoothing cash flows over programme life for the next-gen NB engine
