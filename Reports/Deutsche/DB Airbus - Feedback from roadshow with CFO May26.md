#report #airbus

**Deutsche Bank — Airbus: Feedback from roadshow with CFO, 12 May 2026 — BUY, PT €226** · Christophe Menard, Sriram Krishnan

# Summary
<span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">Constructive tone from CFO Thomas Toepfer: guidance achievable, but conditional on Pratt & Whitney.</span> Delivery profile heavily back-end loaded; next catalyst flagged as the 21 July Business Update.

# Key takeaways
- ~20 China aircraft delayed by certification admin now scheduled through Q2
- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">Pratt GTF commitments for 2026–27 sit outside Airbus' corridor</span> (AOG from the powder-metal recall). Two-year contract breach and dispute; out-of-court deal preferred, downside seen as limited
- CFM talks constructive, but no meaningful extra volume before 2027
- <span style="color:rgb(255, 0, 0)">Rate stability at 75/month could lift profitability</span> (pricing discipline, lower WC)
- FY26 EBIT bridge: 2c FX hedge drag, Spirit costs (low triple-digit €m), R&D, offset by volume
- Supply chain normal except business-class seats (mostly BFE); issues localised to Pratt, not systemic. No gliders in Q1; no Middle East cancellations/deferrals
- Spirit in-housing is a one-off (wings/fuselage are core competence), not a general insourcing policy
- Buybacks "remain a tool in the toolbox"
- Space carve-out for the Bromo JV is complex; preference for closing in 2027
- Defence: strong Q1 order intake; FCAS mediation unresolved; <span style="color:rgb(255, 192, 0)">Eurofighter deliveries to rise from ~13 to ~20/year</span>
- Hedging can't go much further out due to cost

# Relevant data
- Price at report €175.48; PT €226 unchanged
- Medium-term drivers cited: operating leverage, pricing, A350 volumes, favourable A320 mix
